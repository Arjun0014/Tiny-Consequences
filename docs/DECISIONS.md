# Decisions log

Short records of decisions that change the design, scope, budgets or architecture. Newest at the bottom. Format:

```
## D-NNN · YYYY-MM-DD · Title
Context: why a decision was needed.
Decision: what we chose.
Consequences: what changes / what to watch.
```

Claude Code decides autonomously and records here instead of stopping to ask, except where CLAUDE.md says to stop.

## D-001 · 2026-09-27 · Scope and pillars fixed before building
Context: Design discussion between Arjun and Claude.
Decision: Living planet sim on an icosphere graph, statistical fields per cell, individual humans only, fixed weekly tick (48/year), blame-tag causality with chain popups, resilient baseline, 1 real hour = 1 world year offline, deep time via epoch mode, plates don't move.
Consequences: See docs 01–08.

## D-002 · 2026-09-27 · Tiny Skies is reference only
Context: The Tiny Skies repository has no licence.
Decision: No code or assets are copied from it. Visual mood only.
Consequences: All models are generated procedurally or come from clearly licensed CC0/OFL sources.

## D-003 · 2026-09-27 · Sim is pure Lua 5.1, tested headless with LuaJIT
Context: HTML5 runs Lua 5.1.4; Claude Code needs a fast loop that doesn't require the Defold editor.
Decision: `sim/` has no Defold dependencies, uses only Lua 5.1 features, runs under LuaJIT for tests, with an in-browser self-test for HTML5 determinism.
Consequences: View/platform code lives only in `game/`.

## D-004 · 2026-09-27 · SIM_LEVEL 3 (642 cells), provisional
Context: HTML5 release build benchmark (`npm run bench:web -- --release`, headless Chromium, dev machine) with weather + hydrology + vegetation + fire + biome implemented: level 4 = 18.9 ms/tick median, level 3 = 4.6 ms. The HTML5 runtime is interpreted Lua 5.1 in wasm (~25 ns/bytecode), so level 4 can't reach the 4 ms budget at weekly ticks even after optimisation (it would need ~60 bytecodes per cell per tick for *all* systems).
Decision: SIM_LEVEL = 3. Keep code lean and use multi-rate updates for slow systems so the full Chapter-1 tick fits ~4 ms at level 3. Re-benchmark after Stage 1.7; if a finer grid fits, an intermediate geodesic frequency (f=12, 1,442 cells) is possible via grid.lua. The coarse sim is hidden by the level 5–6 render mesh (interpolated elevation + noise for organic coastlines).
Consequences: headless runs are ~4× faster (200 y ≈ 6–10 s), which helps tuning. Spatial rates are scaled by cell spacing (D-005). Villages farm within 1 ring at level 3.

## D-005 · 2026-09-27 · Spatial rates scale with cell spacing
Context: 02_WORLD_MODEL.md §2 requires spatial rates per unit distance. Switching level 4 → 3 without scaling removed all deserts (moisture travelled twice as far per tick).
Decision: `W.sscale = spacing / spacing_L4`. Config values stay tuned per level-4 cell; systems divide advection Courant numbers and fire spread by sscale, diffusion by sscale², recolonisation by sscale, and multiply per-cell lightning by sscale² (per unit area). Cell-count thresholds (wildfire ≥ 10 cells) are converted to level-4 area equivalents (≥ 3 cells at level 3).
Consequences: tuning bands hold across levels (verified at level 4 and level 3 on 4 seeds × 200 y).

## D-006 · 2026-09-27 · Per-cell state outside the field budget
Context: floods need a "bankfull" reference; neighbour reads of grass/forest need last tick's values.
Decision: hydrology keeps `S.hydro.flow_mean` (EMA of flow, persistent system state, not a causal field). grass and forest get prev-tick buffers (spatial=true); that isn't a new field. Transient per-tick scratch arrays (evaporation, melt, runoff) live in `W.scratch` and are never saved.
Consequences: the field catalogue stays at the 24 fields of §3.

## D-007 · 2026-09-27 · Blame implementation details
Context: 03_CAUSALITY.md §3 blame per term per cell is too expensive in interpreted Lua if every cell pays.
Decision: (1) fast path: a system calls `blame.step` only when the cell's tag or any term's tag is non-zero, so quiet cells pay nothing; (2) each system blames its own terms when it writes a field (vegetation growth, fauna grazing and fire burning are separate steps on `grass`); (3) formula fields (`temp`, `precip`, `flow`, `silt`) take the tag of their dominant *anomaly driver* (heat_anom vs g_temp vs aerosol; moisture; the upstream contributor) instead of delta terms; (4) global-driven terms use the ledger's top root only if it explains ≥ 40% of the anomaly.
Consequences: cheap, deterministic, and chains still travel through every coupling. P08 (Chapter 2) measures coverage.

## D-008 · 2026-09-27 · Global climate self-calibrates during spin-up
Context: the no-human world must sit at CO₂ ≈ 280 regardless of seed.
Decision: during spin-up CO₂/g_temp are pinned while the world measures its own reference weathering index, ice fraction, ice volume and mean SST (years 10–30). Afterwards weathering = outgassing × (index / reference), so every seed balances at 280 with a working thermostat. Net vegetation growth absorbs CO₂ and burning emits it with the same coefficient, so a stationary biosphere is carbon-neutral.
Consequences: baseline drift comes only from real imbalances (fire regime settling, villagers).

## D-009 · 2026-09-27 · Art direction and batched props
Context: Arjun shared the target look (Tiny Skies screenshot), recorded in docs/ART_DIRECTION.md.
Decision: props (thousands of tiny trees, houses, crops) are baked into ~40–80 chunk meshes with runtime buffers, rebuilt only when a chunk's sim values change; moving things are pooled. This replaces "props as pooled game objects" from 05_ARCHITECTURE.md §7 for static props.
Consequences: 5k–15k trees at a few dozen draw calls; Stage 1.8 builds the chunk mesh pipeline.

## D-010 · 2026-09-27 · Worldgen shapes continents by growth, plates by Voronoi
Context: 2–3 continents ≥ 12% each and 4–8 islands must hold on 20/20 seeds.
Decision: continents grow from separated centres with a noisy priority flood that never touches another landmass. Islands are grown far from land. Plates (warped Voronoi + Euler-pole motion) only shape mountains, trenches, ridges and volcano sites. Attempts are keyed by (seed, attempt).
Consequences: constraints hold by construction (20/20 seeds pass on the first attempt at level 4 and level 3).

## D-011 · 2026-09-27 · Named regions: landmasses and 6–10 seas
Context: events need a place name (03 §2 `region`), and detectors need regional aggregates (boom/crash, drought).
Decision: `sim/worldgen/regions.lua` (worldgen only): one land region per landmass; a continent holding > 40% of all land is split in two (far-apart seeds, graph-distance Voronoi) and named "North X"/"South X" (or East/West). Seas: 6–10 (≈ ocean share × 12) from a seeded centre plus jittered farthest-point centres, assigned by multi-source BFS through the ocean (connected seas); the largest is kind "ocean". `W.static.region[c]` (persistent) + `W.names.regions[id] = {name, kind, continent, cells, centre, part}` + `W.names.continents`; names from `content/names.lua` with rng stream NAMES. `events.emit` reads `W.static.region`. Regions are not rebuilt when the coastline changes (Chapter 3 must do that at a coastline rebuild).
Consequences: at level 3 a sea is ~30–95 cells — too big for "fish collapse in the sea" (see D-014).

## D-012 · 2026-09-27 · Ocean & fauna are multi-rate; per-cell normals live in system state
Context: the HTML5 tick is already over budget (D-004); ocean + fauna + detectors had to stay lean.
Decision: ocean life runs each ocean cell every 8 ticks (dt = 8, staggered: 1/8 of the cells per tick); fauna local dynamics every 4 ticks (dt = 4, staggered); migration per species every 8 ticks with the rate × 8 (grazers and predators on alternate ticks); detectors monthly (floods every tick from hydrology's list). Oil is a sparse active set (ocean + beached shore cells), every tick. System state outside the field budget: `S.ocean.fish_ref` and `S.fauna.hnorm/pnorm` (per-cell 5-year EMAs = "normal", used by release blame and fish-collapse detection), `S.fauna.cull/hunt` (+ tags; village hooks for Stage 1.7), `S.ocean.oil` (active list, saved so its order is reproducible), detector memory in `W.D`. `fish` gets a prev buffer (spatial) for diffusion — no new field. Predator densities are in units 1/3 of grazer units (a×3, t_h/3, e/3), so wolves read as rarer than deer; dynamics are unchanged by the scaling.
Consequences: cost (LuaJIT -joff, isolated): ocean 0.044 + fauna 0.059 + detectors 0.011 = 0.114 ms/tick vs 1.31 ms for the rest (+9%, ≈ +0.4 ms in the HTML5 build). Stage 1.7 must re-benchmark.

## D-013 · 2026-09-27 · Release blame and tag hygiene
Context: with plain delta blame, grazers booming because predators vanished are attributed to baseline (the growth term is tag 0; the missing predation term is 0). Also tags lingered and got reused by unrelated changes.
Decision: (1) release: when a pressure is far below its local normal (predators < 0.5 × their 5-year normal, normal ≥ a minimum) and the pressure carries a tag, the growth it no longer holds back carries that tag; likewise predators starving because prey is < 0.5 × normal carry the prey's tag. (2) Pressure terms (grazing, browsing, raids, predation) carry the animals' tag only while the animals are > 1.25 × their normal. (3) Flows (migration, fish diffusion) pass their tag only when the net inflow is ≥ 5% of the population in that step (equilibrium exchange carries no cause). (4) An upstream cause never overwrites a more-derived event it produced (`blame.descends` on the flat parent array). (5) Natural (parent 0) species events re-tag only the cells that individually crossed. (6) grazers/predators tag hold 48 steps.
Consequences: S02 reads test_remove_predators → population_crash → extinction_regional → population_boom → biome_shift on 10/10 seeds; natural crashes are clean nature roots.

## D-014 · 2026-09-27 · Detector definitions for Stage 1.6
Context: 03 §4 thresholds need concrete measures at level 3.
Decision: population_boom/crash on land regions: area-weighted mean density vs a 5-year EMA of monthly samples (×2 / ÷2; exit ×1.4 / ÷0.75; 2-year cooldown; skipped below a minimum density). extinction_regional per land region (density < 1e-4 after ≥ 1e-3); extinction_global once when a species' planet total is 0. fish_collapse on connected fishing grounds: ocean cells whose fish fell below 50% of their own 5-year normal (exit 75%), cluster ≥ ceil(6/sscale²) (min 2) cells, named by sea region (a whole sea is far too big at level 3); a cluster whose dominant tag is already a fish_collapse only extends that tag. oil_spreading: connected slick (oil ≥ 0.01) ≥ ceil(10/sscale²) (min 3) cells, once per tag. oil_beached: oil ashore (≥ 0.005), once per tag, aggregated per region. drought: a region whose normal soil water is ≥ 0.2 drops below 0.2 and below 75% of normal for 3 months (deserts are not "in drought"). flood: hydrology's flood cells, per-cell 24-tick cooldown. overfishing is selected in Stage 1.7 (needs village catch). Averages are sampled during spin-up; events only when recording.
Consequences: natural population_crash headlines ≈ 0.05–0.1 per year per seed (predator–prey cycles), no spam.

## D-015 · 2026-09-27 · Ocean currents are mass-conserving push weights
Context: 02 §2 describes currents as upstream weights like the wind tables; pull-form advection doesn't conserve mass, and oil mass must be conserved (it only decays and beaches).
Decision: per season, the wind over each ocean cell, with components pointing into land neighbours removed and renormalised (coastal currents → gyres), becomes downstream push weights over ocean neighbours (plus diffusion); self weight = 1 − Σ out ≥ beach fraction. Current vectors are stored per season in `W.currents[s].vx/vy/vz` for the view.
Consequences: oil advection is exactly conservative (unit test); a pull table for other tracers can be derived later if needed.

## D-016 · 2026-09-27 · Derived tables are pinned to the sea level they were built for
Context: a real-world save round trip was not bit-identical (pre-existing; only the toy world was tested): lapse rates, upwind elevation and coast masks were rebuilt on load from the current `G.sea_level`, which drifts slightly every year, while the live world kept its worldgen-time tables.
Decision: persistent `G.sea_built` (0 at worldgen); geo/temperature/moisture rebuilds use it. A coastline rebuild (Chapter 3) sets `G.sea_built = G.sea_level` then calls `world.rebuild_derived`.
Consequences: real-world P02 (save mid-year with oil and culled predators → reload → continue) is bit-identical (unit test test_save_world).

## D-017 · 2026-09-27 · Browsing follows hunger; animals return eaten carbon
Context: removing predators must stop forest recovery (02 §5.9, S02 forest ≤ 0.85× in 8 years) without grazing wrecking the baseline bands; grazing silently removed biomass that vegetation regrowth then absorbed (CO₂ 280 → 240, −0.7 °C over 200 y).
Decision: browsing = b × H × (0.05 + hunger) × f/(f + 0.08), hunger = min(1, H/K_eff): per unit of forest strongest when forest is low, and hungry grazers (near their food limit, i.e. without predators) strip saplings. Eaten biomass (and oil-killed shore grass) is returned as CO₂ with the vegetation coefficient (D-008 carbon neutrality). Small islands start with no animals (they can't swim); predators are seeded on continents only.
Consequences: baseline bands hold (3 seeds × 200 y: forest 0.25–0.42, grassland 0.22–0.33 means); CO₂ drift back to +0.1 °C/200 y.

## D-018 · 2026-09-27 · Jungle soil-water exit threshold
Context: the jungle condition (soil ≥ 0.5) had no exit threshold (02 §0.3), so jungle↔forest flipped every season: ~270 biome_shift events per century on seed 42, forming long flip-flop "chains".
Decision: `BIOME.jungle_soil_exit = 0.35`.
Consequences: jungle↔forest shifts ≈ 8 per century; forest cover (forest + jungle) unchanged.

## D-019 · 2026-09-27 · Performance pass for the full Chapter-1 tick
Context: before villages the HTML5 tick was ≈ 5 ms against the 4 ms budget; wall-clock benchmarks on the dev machine swing 3× while bob builds run in parallel.
Decision: (1) vegetation runs each land cell every 2 ticks (dt = 2; a cell reads its own values from the live field and its neighbours' from the prev buffer, which it publishes after its update); (2) biome classifies a rotating quarter of the cells per tick (dwell counted in ticks); (3) moisture swaps its buffers instead of copying, precomputes the static capacity factor per season, and skips inflow-tag tracking when no moisture tag existed last tick (count kept in `W.S.moist_tags`, so a reloaded world takes the same path); (4) hydrology visits land cells + river mouths only; (5) ice skips cells that cannot change. Cost is measured deterministically with `tools/opcount.lua` (VM instructions per system; browser ≈ 15 ns per instruction, calibrated on the 4.6 ms D-004 measurement).
Consequences: 343k → 225k instructions/tick with villages and ships included (≈ 5.1 → 3.4 ms in the browser). Bands and scenarios unchanged within noise.

## D-020 · 2026-09-27 · Village economy and demography
Context: 02 §5.10 gives the shape; the numbers must give a population of 120–400 with occasional famines, not a famine every year.
Decision: food in person-months (adult 1/month, child 0.6). Farms ≈ farmers × 0.45 level-4 cells (1 ring at level 3), crops planted in spring, grown monthly (temperature bell 8–32 °C, soil water, fertility; drought, frost and beached-oil losses, fire from the fire system, raids from fauna), harvested once from tick-of-year 32 (≤ farmers × 30 person-months). Hungry villages clear extra fields as far as labour allows; exhausted fields (fertility < 0.22) return to the wild when a fresher ring cell is free (shifting cultivation); standing crops count as ground cover against erosion. A granary holds 6 months of needs; the excess spoils at 8% a month. Births are regulated by the depth of the hungry gap (`fs_low`, a minimum of food security that recovers over a year: 0 below 0.5 months, full above 2.5) plus a capacity backstop (1.6 × the land/sea estimate). Famine starts below 1 month of food and ends only when the hungry gap has closed (`fs_low` > 2); migration counts months with `fs_low` < 1. Fishing: catch = min(Σ fish × q, fishers × 2.5/month), removed from the fish (overfishing emerges). Hunting and predator culls go through the fauna hooks. Wood burning + clearing are the villagers' CO₂ source (ledger "villagers"; tuned for ≈ +1.2…+1.5 °C by year 200).
Consequences: S01 quick 3/3; full profile 5/10 (some worlds' populations dip under 120 or overshoot 400: the capacity estimate is the weak point, see PROGRESS).

## D-021 · 2026-09-27 · Entity schema and rng keys for people
Context: the renderer and inspector consume villages, people and ships; rolls must be local (05 §5).
Decision: `W.ent.villages` (array, ids = indices, abandoned villages stay with alive = false, `ruin` while ruins stand), `W.ent.people` (id → person; the dead kept 30 years), `W.ent.ships` (tankers, stable ids), `W.ent.boats` (rebuilt monthly), `W.ent.volcanoes`, `W.static.lanes`, `W.S.ruins` (cell → tick). Schemas are documented in the headers of villages.lua, people.lua and ships.lua. A person's rng key = hash(village founding cell, birth tick, index among that tick's births); monthly rolls are frac(village-month base + u × φᵏ) with u = the person's fixed random fraction (a Weyl offset: one hash per village-month instead of one per person per roll).
Consequences: an extra birth anywhere changes nobody else's fate; the village update costs ≈ 8k instructions/tick.

## D-022 · 2026-09-27 · Worldgen settle inside the spin-up
Context: 02 §2 requires villages, lanes and volcano sites, and a lived-in Year 0.
Decision: `sim/worldgen/settle.lua` runs at spin-up year 15 (15 years before Year 0): cultures per landmass, 3–5 villages (fishing, inland, second continent first, then the best free sites ≥ 3 level-4 steps apart), 150–250 people as households, tanker lanes as closed ocean loops A (next to a coastal village) → far B → far C → A with 1–2 tankers each, 5–10 arc volcano sites on convergent boundaries + 1–3 hotspots (≥ 1 in the ocean). P10 checks the settle report on 20/20 seeds (villages may later die or move — that is turnover, not a worldgen failure).
Consequences: spin-up costs ≈ +0.1 s; the settle report lives in `W.worldgen_report.settle` (not saved).

## D-023 · 2026-09-27 · Level-independent wildfire magnitude; small biome threshold nudges
Context: at level 3 wildfires (≥ 3 cells) never reached the 10-cell headline minimum, so P09 ran at ≈ 0.1 headlines/year; some worlds sat on knife edges of the desert/forest bands.
Decision: wildfire magnitude is reported in level-4-equivalent cells (cells × sscale²). Biome thresholds: desert entry at soil and veg < 0.17 (was 0.15), forest entry/exit 0.45/0.36 (was 0.5/0.4). Fire spread 0.46 (was 0.42), lightning 1.7e-3 (was 1.5e-3), prey refuge 0.02.
Consequences: headline rate 0.23–0.43/year on the S01 seeds; bands hold on the quick seeds.

## D-024 · 2026-09-27 · Test profile changes for Stage 1.7
Context: 07 §4 asks for P06 over 500 years; the quick profile must stay ≈ 3 minutes.
Decision: P06 quick = 1 seed × 200 years (shared with S01), full = 2 seeds × 500 years. S01 is a baseline-only scenario sharing the cached baseline runs (tests/sim/support/baseline.lua). S04 targets `ocean_near:village:fishing` and adds "village food security ≤ 0.7× baseline within 2 years". The S03 chain check only counts events within one year of the fire.

## D-025 · 2026-09-27 · Population regulated by each village's real food history
Context: D-020 regulated births with a crude land/sea capacity estimate: on the full profile populations either ran into the 500 cap or slowly died out on poorer worlds (S01 5/10).
Decision: each village keeps ~3-year EMAs of what it actually produces (harvest + catch + hunting) and eats; their ratio ρ is its food margin and `capacity = pop × ρ / comfort`. Births scale with ρ (10% floor below 1.05, full at 1.35) and with the depth of the hungry gap; a chronic deficit (ρ < 0.95 for 3 months) sends a family away (to a village with food, else founding on the best free land). Supporting changes: villages are sized at settle by their site's capacity; the settle happens at spin-up year 8; emigrating families are a village-month roll (not always the founders); granary 9 months; land yield 66 (was 80) and fish 340 person-months per unit (was 400); farm fertility depletion 0.003 (soils equilibrate instead of drifting down for a century); fishing takes at most 5% of the stock a month (fish_q 20: near the stock's best sustainable yield — at 12.5% catches collapsed over a century); hunger hurts more once stores are gone (health loss 0.4, frailty 0.006). The planet's hard cap of 500 is approached softly: births taper over the last 55% below it.
Evidence (10 seeds × 200 y, full profile): population stays within 120–400 after year 5 on 9/10 seeds (seed 7 dips to 119 in a churn of famines, migrations and refoundings); no runaway, no slow death. S01 7/10, S02 10/10, S03 9/10, S04 9/10.

## D-026 · 2026-09-27 · Compact saves
Context: a year-40 save was 354 KB (people 185 KB as keyed tables, system arrays and events at full precision).
Decision: SAVE_VERSION 2. People and event records travel as positional records in the save (the in-memory schema is unchanged); the per-cell system arrays (animal and fish normals, flow EMA) are snapped to 16 bits with the fields at the year boundary and saved as quantised arrays; people's continuous state (health, hunger, mood, memory salience) is rounded at the same snap (systems may define `snap(W)`, run after the field snap and before the capture); a person's random fraction is quantised at birth; the dead are kept 10 years (was 30). Grazers/predators lost their unused prev buffers.
Consequences: 187 KB at year 40 (seed 2), P02/P03 bit-identical.

## D-027 · 2026-09-27 · Pre-spun default world: seed 3
Context: generating + spinning up a world costs ~11 s in the browser; 02 §2 asks for a pre-spun default world.
Decision: `tools/make_world.lua --seed 3 --out assets/worlds/default.tcsave` saves the Year-0 world (the same year-boundary snap the first tick performs, then the capture); the game loads it with `require("sim.worlds").from_save_string(str)` (≈ 15× faster than generating; 158 KB). ## D-028 · 2026-09-27 · Vegetation bands on natural land; local population booms; threshold evidence
Context: villages, farms and ruins occupy 5–9% of the land at level 3 and count as neither forest nor grassland, pushing dry worlds under the 20% floors; population_boom only looked at whole-region averages, so hunting near villages could hide a real boom on part of a landmass (S02 seed 3).
Decision: S01 measures the §10 vegetation bands (forest, grassland, desert) as shares of natural land (land minus villages, farms, ruins; metrics forest_nat/grass_nat/desert_nat; human_land reported). population_boom also fires for a connected patch (≥ 10 level-4 cells) where a species exceeds 2.2× its own 5-year normal (sharing the regional cooldown). Classification: forest entry/exit 0.42/0.34 (D-023 had 0.45/0.36); lightning 1.9e-3; prey refuge 0.018 (headline rate ≥ 0.2/year on 10/10 seeds).
Evidence: natural-land forest on the 10 seeds 0.20–0.40, grassland 0.21–0.42, desert 0.06–0.24; seed 2 (17.5% desert) sits at forest 0.199 — geography, left failing.

## D-029 · 2026-09-27 · P07 measures VM instructions, not wall time
Context: 07 §4 P07 needs a regression gate on per-system tick cost. Wall-clock timings on the dev machine swing 3× with concurrent load (bob builds, browsers), which makes a 30% gate either flaky or blind.
Decision: P07 counts Lua VM instructions per tick per system (debug count hook, LuaJIT interpreter with the JIT switched off and flushed while measuring) on a fixed world (seed 42, level 3, one year after spin-up, 192 ticks), via `tools/perf.lua`. The committed `tests/bench-baseline.json` holds the per-system counts, date and calibration; the test fails when a system grows > 30% (and by > 2,000 instructions, so tiny systems don't trip on a few lines) or the whole tick > 15%. Intentional updates: `npm run bench -- --update-baseline` (plus a DECISIONS entry); `npm run bench -- --check` runs the gate alone. Calibration against the real target: the HTML5 release build ran the `sim/bench.lua` configuration at 1.70 ms median for 230,910 instructions/tick, i.e. ≈ 7.4 ns per instruction (6–10 ns per system). This supersedes the ≈ 15 ns estimate in D-019, which came from the older 4.6 ms (D-004) measurement.
Consequences: exact and repeatable (0.6 s); it counts LuaJIT bytecodes, not PUC Lua 5.1's, and C calls count as one instruction, so it tracks cost changes in Lua code, not constant-factor differences between runtimes — re-check the calibration with `npm run bench:web -- --release` at chapter ends.

## D-030 · 2026-09-27 · SIM_LEVEL 3 finalised
Context: 02 §2 asks to finalise SIM_LEVEL from a browser benchmark of the realistic tick (after 1.5). With all Chapter-1 systems (weather, hydrology, vegetation, fire, biome, ocean, fauna, villages, ships, detectors) after the 1.7 performance pass, `npm run bench:web -- --release` measures **1.70 ms median, 2.5 ms p95** per tick at level 3 in the HTML5 release build (headless Chromium). Level 4 would be ≈ 4× (≈ 6.8 ms), over the 4 ms budget.
Decision: SIM_LEVEL = 3 is final for the jam. The headroom (~2×) is reserved for Chapter 2–3 systems (powers, geology, epoch mode) and lets ⏩ run ~4 ticks per frame.
Consequences: P07 (D-029) guards the budget.

## D-031 · 2026-09-27 · Terrain and sea are one mesh coloured from a per-cell texture
Context: 05 §7 describes separate terrain and ocean meshes with per-vertex colours rewritten incrementally.
Decision: one level-5 render icosphere (61k vertices) carries land and sea. Each render vertex interpolates elevation from its 3 nearest sim cells, and land/sea comes from an interpolated coast value plus noise, so coasts are organic despite the 642-cell sim. Colours come from a small per-cell texture (2 px per sim cell) sampled in the vertex shader. A recolour is one pass over 642 cells (≤ 4×/s, amortised) plus one texture upload, not a rewrite of 61k vertices.
Consequences: cheap updates; sea-level changes (Chapter 3) rebuild the mesh in one step, which is acceptable because it's rare.

## D-032 · 2026-09-27 · Props: procedural Lua meshes in 80 chunks; budget raised
Context: D-009 chose batched chunk meshes. 06 §1 suggested a Node→glTF generator and 05 §9 budgeted ≤ 2,500 props.
Decision: prop geometry (tree species, bushes, tufts, rocks, stumps, fields, houses, huts, ruins) is generated procedurally in Lua at startup and baked into 80 chunk meshes. No glTF pipeline: the chunks need raw triangles anyway. Budget: ~12k props baked, ≤ ~9.5k visible, 37–50 real draw calls at the planet view. Near chunks switch to richer templates when the camera zooms in.
Consequences: the dense "miniature forest" look (ART_DIRECTION.md) within the draw-call budget.

## D-033 · 2026-09-27 · Movers, fire and batching
Decision: moving things (clouds, deer, wolves, people, boats, tankers, fire/smoke pieces) are pooled game objects in world-space-vertex materials, so the engine batches them into one draw call per material. They draw in their own unsorted render predicate, because depth sorting between chunks broke the batches. Fire and smoke use pooled unlit meshes, because Defold particles don't face the camera for this use.
Consequences: ≤ 300 movers at a handful of draw calls.

## D-034 · 2026-09-27 · UI built in code, fonts, HTML template
Decision: the HUD and inspector are built in code on a Defold GUI scene and batched by layers (119 → 47 draw calls). They use Fredoka and Nunito static instances cut from the Google Fonts variable fonts (OFL, `assets/fonts`; Fredoka has no "₂" glyph, so CO₂ uses Nunito), and a custom HTML template so the canvas fills the window with no footer strip. HUD animal numbers are density sums shown with fixed display scales (presentation only).

## D-035 · 2026-09-27 · Camera framing
Decision: a trackball orbit with no pole lock. The default zoom is closer than "whole planet with margin": the planet fills ~85% of the frame height, like the reference image. Zooming out still reaches the whole planet. The camera idles in a slow auto-rotate.

## D-036 · 2026-09-27 · Villages farm by their own season; sites must be able to feed a village
Context: on the default world Rialora (49°S) starved to abandonment in Years 3–4 with no cause in its chain. Traced month by month, its crop *shrank* every season: the crop calendar (plant before tick-of-year 12, harvest from 32) is the northern one, so every southern village planted in autumn and grew its crops through winter (−10 °C, frost). Separately, settle accepted the site because scoring used the *annual mean* temperature (2.4 °C passes the ≥ 2 gate) while its fields could never ripen. This bug, not population regulation, was behind most of the famine/abandonment churn (seed 7: +24/−26 villages in 200 years).
Decision: (1) each village farms on its own calendar: `villages.season_shift` = 24 ticks south of the equator (derived, `W.vgeo[id].shift`); land use (shifting cultivation) and crops use it. (2) `site_score` scales by growing-season warmth (the crop temperature bell at summer warmth, shared with `capacity`); `villages.pick_site` takes the best-scoring free site whose land and sea can feed ≥ `min_site_people` (20). Worldgen settle uses it (falling back to the best score) and so do migrants founding villages. (3) Planting: an exhausted field (fertility < fallow_fert) rests only when the store can afford it (food security ≥ `fallow_fs` = 6 months); a hungry village also breaks poor ground (≥ `poor_fert` 0.05). Capacity already assumed those fields are farmed.
Consequences: the default world keeps all its villages; churn on seed 7 falls to a handful of foundings/abandonments per 200 years.

## D-037 · 2026-09-27 · Soils reach equilibrium during spin-up (accelerated fertility spin-up)
Context: fertility relaxes toward its vegetation/erosion equilibrium with a ~40-year time constant, but spin-up is 30 years and vegetation is (re)seeded at year 4. Every world started on "painted" soils and then lost fertility for 60–80 years (share of land below the fallow line: 13% → 38%). Villages sized at settle then slowly starved (seed 10: Venmere's only two fields eroded from 0.45 to 0.0).
Decision: during spin-up (`W.G.calibrating`), fertility regeneration and erosion's fertility loss run `SPINUP_FERT_BOOST` = 8× faster (silt itself is unchanged). This is the standard accelerated spin-up for slow pools: same equilibrium, reached before Year 0. Vegetation had been tuned against the richer painted soils, so the regeneration target is raised to `fert_base = {0.3, 0.8}` (was {0.25, 0.75}), restoring similar effective fertility.
Evidence: land fertility is flat from Year 0 (seed 3: 0.398 → 0.395 over 100 y). By biome: forest 0.73, jungle 0.75, farmland 0.61, grassland 0.37, desert 0.19, tundra 0.15, barren 0.11.

## D-038 · 2026-09-27 · Daughter villages, a soft population ceiling, village lineage
Context: once the calendar bug was fixed, villages almost never founded new ones (only famine migrants did), so worlds slowly lost villages and never regained them. Deep time would hollow out the planet, and 02 §7 chain 8 (human growth) needs the human footprint to spread.
Decision: a village with ≥ `bud_pop` (60) people, ≥ `bud_fs` (5) months of food and a food margin under `bud_rho` (1.25: its land is filling and births are slowing) rolls monthly (`bud_prob` 0.02, at most once per `bud_years` = 15) to send `bud_share` (20%) of its people, young families with adults ≤ 35, to found a village at a site that can feed ≥ 20 (`pick_site`). This happens while the planet has < `bud_total` (330) people and < `max_villages` (8) villages. Events: migration (nature, `colonists`) → village_founded (headline). Famine migration shares the founding code (`found_from`). Villages record `from` (the village their founders came from). Births now taper to 0 at `global_soft` = 450 (the hard cap of 500 is unchanged), which keeps populations inside the 120–400 band with margin (full profile: 213–352).
Consequences: 1–8 villages founded and 0–5 abandoned per 200 years per seed. Year 0 can hold up to ~9 villages, because budding also happens in the 22 spin-up years after settle (P10 checks the settle itself).

## D-039 · 2026-09-27 · Interannual weather, floods and droughts that actually happen
Context: the baseline climate was almost a perfect loop (annual precipitation CV ≈ 0.015, temperature wobble sd ≈ 0.2 °C). The flood detector had never fired on any seed (rivers peak at 0.22 while the rule needed > 0.3), and the regional drought detector never fired either (a whole landmass had to average < 0.2 soil water). 02 §8 asks for "noise with memory".
Decision: (1) An ocean-evaporation wobble, like Earth's sea-surface oscillations: 2 smooth planet-scale patterns (unit peak) whose strengths drift, with knots every 2 years, scale sea evaporation by up to ±`rain_wobble_amp` (0.6). Moisture is conserved, so the land downwind gets wet and dry spells (annual precipitation CV median ≈ 0.07, p90 ≈ 0.18). A rain-out capacity wobble was tried first and did nothing, because rain here is supply-limited. (2) Floods (02 §5.5): bankfull = EMA of each river's yearly peak (the mean annual flood; persistent `hydro.bankfull` and `hydro.flow_peak`, 16-bit snapped like `flow_mean`). A flood is flow > 2 × bankfull on a river ≥ 0.05; it spills onto land neighbours up to 50 m higher (the floodplain), and overbank water leaves silt (+fertility). Thresholds of 1.15–1.5× flooded riverside villages almost every year. In villages, a flood drowns 60% of the standing crop on farm cells (blamed on the flood), and a flood through the village emits `flooded_village` (headline, at most once per 5 years per village). (3) Drought detector per cell: seasonal soil-water normals (four 5-year EMAs); a cell is dry below 70% of its season's normal for 3 months; a drought is a connected dry patch ≥ 12 level-4-equivalent cells. It re-tags soil water, so crop failures chain to it. None of this adds a causal field.
Evidence (10 seeds × 200 y): floods 8–105, droughts 1–39 and village floods 1–41 (before the 5-year news cap) per seed; wildfires now occur in every 50-year window of the old default world.

## D-040 · 2026-09-27 · Headline hygiene for booms, crashes and floods
Context: ~60% of all headlines on every seed were grazer `population_crash` events at 0.48–0.50 of their 5-year normal: the ordinary deer-cycle trough, recorded at the moment it crossed ÷2. The default world announced the same two regions' deer crash every ~5 years.
Decision: (1) A boom/crash event tracks its peak/trough while it lasts (its magnitude is updated; `D.fauna[sp].ev`). (2) Event kinds may set `hmax` / `hmax_nature` (a headline only when the magnitude is ≤ it). A crash with a cause is news at ÷2 (`hmax` 0.5, so culls stay headlines); a natural one only below 0.33. (3) News fatigue: a region's natural boom/crash headlines come at most once per `news_years` (25) per species (`D.fauna[sp].news`). Events are always recorded, so chains and "Why?" are unaffected; only the headline flag changes.
Consequences: crashes are ≈ 25–40% of headlines; headline rates are 0.17–0.42/year across seeds, with wildfires, floods, booms, famines and village turnover making up the rest.

## D-044 · 2026-09-27 · Colonists that can survive: timing, provisions, jobs, orphans
Context: reading chains on seed 7 and seed 5 after D-038 showed failing colonies. Colonists could leave at any time of year with ~4 months of food, so a village founded after planting waited up to 18 months for a harvest (seed 7: 23 → 6 people, then one child alone for three years). Settlers were given jobs before the new village knew its fishing waters, so a one-cell islet got 12 farmers and no fishers (seed 5, "New Hope"), and even with fishers the 45%-of-workers fishing cap left half of a landless village idle. Rusty Islet was resettled six times in 200 years.
Decision: (1) Colonists set out only in early spring by the *destination's* calendar (local tick-of-year < plant_toy − 4), so they plant on arrival; they carry `start_food` (8) months of provisions from the parent's store (at most half of it). (2) A new village computes its fishing jobs from its waters before settlers are assigned jobs. (3) With no fields to farm, farmers take to the boats and the fishing-share cap is lifted. (4) When only children are left, the village they came from (else the nearest village with food) takes them in (migration, `orphans`), and the village is abandoned. (5) Village fission also happens by size: a village of ≥ `bud_big` (100) people may send colonists whatever its food margin (rich worlds' villages never get "crowded": births are held back by the planet's soft ceiling, not by land). Colonists' monthly chance is 0.1 in the two eligible spring months (≈ the old yearly rate).
Evidence (10 seeds × 200 y): colonies founded after Year 0 mostly take root (one abandoned within 10 years across all seeds); Rusty Islet's colony lives 200 years; turnover in 10/10 seeds.

## D-052 · 2026-09-28 · W2 (in progress): the settlement layer's first calibration
Context: implementing docs/world/11–13 (`sim/society/`: entities, sites, layout, farming, fishing, labour, construction, economy, life, mobility, init) and running it headless on seeds 22, 3, 7, 1, 5, 9 for 80 years (`tools/society_trace.lua`). Decision (calibration and mechanisms found necessary): (1) Fields: yield 4,000 FU per fully grown cell (in practice ~1,900–2,500 per sown field), labour sow 450 / tend 300 / harvest 600 h per cell (~15 person-days per person fed), an 8-week spring sowing window with children helping, ripeness needs degree days *and* a grown crop; villages learn their real yield per sown field (EMA) and plan fields with it; they avoid cells that flooded in the last two years; a bad year never makes them release fields wholesale (one field a week, only when clearly too many); Chayanov restraint: with > 1.5 years of grain they crop fewer fields. (2) Fishing: q = 2.2 FU per crew-hour at density 1 (≈ 8 FU a fisher-day), squalls 0.02% of trips; boats wear with use and are mended in port; piers are repaired; a fishing village with no boat builds one. (3) Rationing counts the usual catch expected before the next harvest, and the harvest date comes from the village's own season (degree days / last harvest week). (4) The preventive check: a betrothed couple marries only with a home *and* a livelihood — the village's carrying capacity (reachable land cropped with fallow at its learned yield + 8% of the fish stock a year) must exceed its population by 5%; otherwise they wait, and crowded villages send young couples to found daughter villages (every ≥ 6 years, spring). Farm radius 3 steps (walking cost in the field choice). (5) Grassland adaptation: where no woods are within 6 cells people gather brush, dung and straw for fuel (grass patch, no timber) and build earth houses (18 WU, 360 h). (6) Fertility: daily conception 0.005, nursing 18 months, marriage from 19 (women) / 21 (men). (7) Performance: per-cell weekly weather cache (one hash per cell-week + a 32-bit LCG), cached distances, weekly ranking of fishing grounds, a pregnancy list, fuel and storage caches; labour re-planned only when a task has nothing left. P07 now profiles hourly systems (`villages:hour`).
Consequences: 80-year runs on six seeds: 183–629 people, 12–18 villages founded and 7–14 abandoned (high churn — next tuning target), chains through fire, floods, boats, seed, famine, migration and abandonment. Society ≈ 390k VM instructions per simulated week at ~375 people (planet ≈ 500k): over the 1.5 ms target, to be profiled in the browser (W6). Tests, saves, pre-spun worlds, view and tools are not yet ported (SESSION_HANDOFF.md). ## D-064 · 2026-09-28 · Generational memory of hunger (dread)
Context: villages planned their fields and stores the same whether or not they had starved ten years before; memories were personal only (mood), faded in a few years, and changed no decision.
Decision:
1. **Memory**: the worst things are remembered longest — a memory's yearly fade is `memory_fade^(1 − 0.95 × min(1, 1.3 × salience))` (a famine, salience 0.9, is ~0.8 after ten years, ~0.7 after twenty, ~0.3 after thirty, gone after forty: a generation; a smoky week is forgotten in a year or two). Friends remember a friend's death (half a family member's grief).
2. **Dread** (`economy.dread`, monthly): the mean over the village's grown-ups (16+) of each one's strongest memory of going hungry (famine 1.0, seed eaten 0.7, a failed harvest 0.45, rationing 0.3, × the memory's salience), 0..1, and the event most of them remember (`v.dread_ev`).
3. **What it changes** (`farming.field_want`, used by `farming.plan`): the field margin × (1 + 0.2 dread) and the store a village is content with (Chayanov's fallow threshold) 1.5 + dread years of grain. So a famine makes the next generation careful; as its survivors die the caution fades and the risk returns — a cycle nobody scripted. Inspector: the village's remembered hunger ("They remember the famine of year 73 (dread 62%): they sow more and keep more in store"); the farm workers' Why mentions it.
4. **Evidence** (100-year runs, 2,400 cap, famines / rationing events): seed 3 — with dread 6 / 52, without 7 / 67, store effect only 8 / 60; seed 9 — with dread (margin 0.35) 10 / 106, without 2 / 79, store only 5 / 98. Single runs diverge chaotically and famines are few: the effect is not established either way; the margin effect was halved (0.35 → 0.2: a weakened village sowing more than its hands can work is the likelier harm). B17 checks the mechanism (memories → dread → the store a village is content with).

## D-066 · 2026-09-28 · Ties that fade, bad blood from splits, rivals for pasture and fishing grounds (session 5)

Diagnosis (`tools/raid_diag.lua`, per pair: the relation score's parts, distance or sea cost, attackers, defence with allies, pwin, gain, the gate): every relation only ever grew. Kin was a ratchet (`r.kin = max(r.kin, this month's count)`: in-laws dead for decades still bound two villages), past trades counted forever (capped at 5), the parent/daughter tie never fell below 0.3, the reciprocity ledger never forgot, and a lineage that split away after a contested succession was welcomed as a loving daughter. ## D-071 · 2026-09-28 · Wild ecology v2: cycles from body condition, winters, territories and dispersal — on branch `wip/wild-agents`, not yet merged (session 6)
Context: D-070's herds and packs damped the wolf-deer cycles (continental predator CV 0.05). Diagnosis (`tools/wild_dyn.lua`): each pack fluctuated (yearly CV 0.28) but packs were unrelated (pairwise r 0.004), so ~24 packs a continent averaged the cycles away; the field model on main is synchronous only through an unrealistically strong cell-to-cell diffusion (patch synchrony 0.80-0.91 at every distance). Winter snow covers only ~18% of land and varies ~10% a year (correlated 0.79 → 0.47 with distance); lean-season forage varies ~14% (0.50 → 0.13).
Decision (branch commits 9618825, d5bae5c; design in `docs/world/14_ECOLOGY.md` §5 there):
1. **Body condition** (fat reserves) for herds and packs; forage per head = min(1.5, food / (0.6 H + 0.02)) − 0.8 × snow; no logistic term (density dependence through food per head). **Fawns** once a year (1.4 a head × the herd's lowest condition since the last fawning: a hard winter is paid for in the next spring, Mech et al. 1987); **one litter** a pack a year (5 × condition); starvation below thresholds; whole wolves (drawn births and deaths).
2. **Predation** × (1 + 1.5 snow) × (0.7 + 0.75 weakness) — wolves kill 2-3× as often in deep snow and take the weak; a pack's kills grow with (size/5)^0.6 (per-wolf intake falls with pack size, Schmidt & Mech 1997).
3. **Territories** (no den within two steps of another), a territory's **small game** (feeds ~1.2 wolves), **dispersal** in the autumn or when starving (keep 2 + 6 × condition; 60% survive; settle toward prey per wolf within 7 steps: a lone non-kin mate, free ground, or a pack with room), **lone wolves roam 8 steps for a mate** (kin only after a year alone), starving packs move dens toward prey; herds' **fission-fusion** (merge on the same ground) and **hunger browsing** (what the grass lacks, taken from trees at 0.5 of its worth).

## D-074 · 2026-10-01 · Memory of hunger with real levers: granaries against the famine, obligations, repayment, surplus gifts, feasts (session 8)
Context: D-072 found that the memory of hunger (dread, D-064) could not matter: the store it asked for (1.5 + dread years) could not exist — granaries held 0.5-1 year of need (at most 1 + pop/40 of them), villages sat at capacity most months and 30-67% of a harvest could rot. Decision:
1. **The store a village is content with** (`SOCIETY.store_years` 1.5, + `dread_store` × dread): granaries are built while the store overflows until they hold it (the old cap of a granary per 40 people is gone), and beyond it nobody sows more (Chayanov, as before, at the same 1.5 years). A village that remembers hunger wants more: a granary it builds beyond what others keep carries the remembered famine as its cause (`granary_built`, parented on the famine most of its grown-ups remember). The institution outlives the memory: the granaries stand (and are repaired) after the generation that starved is gone.
2. **Obligation** (the ledger in FU, each side reckoning it in its own need-days): a village that took food and has not returned it is bound to the giver (`owe_w` 0.004 per need-day of its own, up to 250) — better disposed to it in every choice that reads the relation (relief, trade partners, brides, allies, the friend rule that spares villages from raids); the giver's resentment of unreturned gifts is now reckoned in the giver's need-days (what a gift cost a big village is little; what it meant to a small one, much).
3. **Repayment**: a village asked for relief by one it owes gives at least what it took (as far as three-quarters of its spare allows); the relief is marked `repay`.
4. **Surplus**: grain beyond what the granaries hold would rot. Each month the village carries it (its porters) to a friendly village with room — first one it owes (the gift carries the relief it repays as its cause: famine > relief > gift), else the one with the fewest days of food; what is still left is eaten at a harvest feast once a year, with guests from the friendly villages of its landmass (they are bound to the host by what they ate) and the leader's household honoured (`feast_prestige`).
5. The chronicle tells it: "Fed in our hunger by X", "Sent grain to X in their hunger, repaying our debt", "Sent our surplus to X, who fed us in our hunger", "A harvest feast (N villages our guests)", "Built a granary against the memory of the famine".

## D-080 · 2026-10-01 · The year made real: a three-field rotation, labour at pre-industrial scale, cooking, hearths, tools, wild food that runs out (session 10)
Context: the first whole-year watch (`tools/v3_year.lua`, 54 days) read as artificial: all 120 rows hoed and sown in two days, then adults spent ~30% of the waking day foraging (in 0.1-0.8 FU round trips) and ~30% resting; the fields plus the forage yielded 2.3x what the village ate; no wood burned above 8 °C (a warm site never lit a fire); apprentices and elders mostly talked; a fallow field could be sown again the week it was harvested; carried loads were overwritten when a plan changed (food lost); the field planner assumed 1 FU a person a day while people ate ~0.7.
Decision (mechanisms, not appearances):
1. **Rotation**: every plot runs winter crop (sown in the autumn window, local days 26-35; it all but rests in winter: `winter_dormant`) → spring crop (sown days 0-13) → a year's rest (ploughed in summer, days 12-25, against the weeds; the soil recovers). Fertility per plot: a crop takes `fert_crop`, rest and ploughing give it back; yield × fertility. Founding: 12 plots, a third in each state.
2. **Rows are worked in stints with their progress kept** (`row_p`): hoeing 7 h a row, reaping 5 h, sowing 1.2 h (× skill, × tools); a started row is finished first by whoever comes. Threshing 6 FU an hour, under the granary roof (rain makes it the job of the day; winter too). Houses ~110 work-hours and 18 logs.
3. **Cooking**: pantry grain becomes meals at the hearth (2.4 h a batch, one cook per household; nobody cooks without wood on the hearth); meals go stale. Hearths burn wood every day (`fuel_cook`), more below 16 °C; autumn and winter lay in `winter_wood_days` of burning.
4. **Wild food** at the woods' edge regrows on its standing trees by season and is picked out (`s.wild`); foraging is a supplement (~25% of the diet in late summer) and shrinks as the woods are cleared.
5. **Tools** wear with field, wood and building work and slow it when worn; households mend them at home or by the door (a little wood). **Houses** weather and are repaired (a log, 2.5 h).
6. **Stores**: grain, sheaves and pantry food spoil slowly. **Loads in hand** are put away before an unrelated plan (seed kept for field work). **The field planner** uses the village's measured eating (meals + bread in the fields), not 1 FU/day.
7. Leisure: talkers stay where they stand; a crowd at the well puts the quieter ones off (they sit by their own door); cold evenings are spent visiting by the hearth.
Test premise restated: `test_v3_agents.four_days` — the waiting couple's house now stands within 12 days (it was 4 when a house was 26 work-hours), and within 4 days its site has logs and hours worked.
Numbers (seed 22, 54 days): food conserved to 0.01 FU every day (harvest 1,092 FU, forage 331, eaten 1,152, spoiled ~190); 131 trees felled; adults' waking day ~45% work in spring and summer (rest 21-24%), winter still social (talk/visit ~50%: textiles and livestock, the real winter work, are not modelled yet).

## D-081 · 2026-10-01 · Life stages with real care: infants held and fed, minders, children who tag along, apprentices beside a mentor; households work their own fields; spare hands clear land (session 10)
Context: after D-080 children still played ~88% of the day, apprentices and elders mostly talked and rested, infants were "fed" by a constant, all-hands bursts did the village's field work in a day, and a couple moving into their new house left a stale cooking claim that starved the two people left behind (found by the year watch).
Decision (`sim/agents/kin.lua`, think/act/init/life):
1. **Infants** are always held by somebody (`carer` = the person whose position and building they share; that person's `babe`). They get hungry (faster awake than asleep) and call for food: their nursing mother is interrupted wherever she is (she nurses where she stands, comes to whoever minds the baby, and wakes for it at night); a weaned toddler is fed porridge from the home's meals by whoever holds it (who cooks first if nothing is cooked). A baby nobody feeds loses health. Holding a baby rules out felling, building and hauling and slows field work (`BABE_FIT`); to work, a mother leaves her baby with a **minder** at home (a grandparent, an older child, the father at home) and takes it back when she is home again.
2. **Children** play (siblings meet at the household's play spot of the day; hours of play pall), **tag along** with a parent working nearby (the youngest most), and from 8-9 fetch water and firewood (a log at a time; a few chores a day). Never field hands (test).
3. **Apprentices** get a **mentor** (the parent of their sex with a craft, else the other parent, a household adult, kin; founders too, and a new one when theirs dies) and take a hand in the mentor's own work on the same target (the same plot, building, the trees the mentor fells: hoeing, building and threshing only beside the mentor), learning faster there.
4. **Elders** tire faster at work, keep an eye on grandchildren at play, cook and mend tools more.
5. **Own fields**: a household works its own plots; another household's only when that one falls behind late in the window; the harvest is reaped together. Field work is paced by each household's hands, not all-hands bursts.
6. **Spare hands clear land**: the village measures adults' idle share of the day (`idle_avg`); above `idle_expand` (0.4) it stakes out new field ground even when the food it wants is covered (up to 1.6x): the fields grow with the labour (12 → 17 plots in the first year).
7. Household chore claims hold only while their holder is a living member still at that objective (`claim_free`).
8. A fresh v3 world opens at its first dawn (the sim lives through the founding night).
Numbers (seed 22, 54 days): no deaths; food conserved exactly; harvest 1,502 FU; 174 trees felled; children's summer: play 57%, tagging along 26%; adults' spring/summer ~60% busy; winter still social (textiles and livestock are the missing winter work). Tests: `tests/sim/unit/test_v3_year.lua` (economy closes, nobody starves, infants held and fed, children never field hands, apprentices beside mentors, the rotation turns).

## D-086 · 2026-10-01 · Catch-up speed: cheaper paths and decisions, same behaviour (session 11)

## D-087 · 2026-10-01 · Settlements on both sides of the equator; crops by climate; fishing as an occupation (session 11)
Context: worldgen founded only in the north (day 0 is the northern spring and the founding states assumed it). Allowing the south exposed three mechanism faults: sites were judged by the founding day's temperature (a southern autumn reading: two founded sites had annual means under 8 °C); crop development saturated only at 15 °C, so in 10-13 °C climates a spring crop took ~40 days and an autumn-sown winter crop a whole year; development was also slowed by dry soil (a dry site's crop never ripened, where in reality it ripens on time with a thin yield). Five coastal villages then showed that fishing had no economics: landed fish went into the pantry as if it were grain and every household fished whenever a boat was free (1,500-1,700 FU each in 54 days, more than the harvest).
Decision:
1. **Founding by the local season**: each field's place in the three-field rotation is derived from the local day of year (a three-year cycle per field: winter crop sown on local day 30, reaped in early summer; stubble to spring; spring crop sown and reaped as autumn begins; a year's rest ploughed in summer), reproducing the old northern spring state exactly; granary and woodstore stocks follow the local season (fuller in autumn). A southern settlement founded on day 0 is in its autumn: a third of its fields ripe, a third ploughed for the winter sowing two days away.
2. **Sites**: judged by the annual mean temperature (`temp_static`), passes 10-19 °C then 8.5-23 °C; settlements after the first may be coastal or river-side in any pass and score water access (+0.6 a shore, +0.5 a river); the first stays a northern dawn site. Seed 22, `--settlements 6`: 6 settlements, 145 people on 3 landmasses (1 north, 5 south), 5 with docks, 5 with rivers on their maps.
3. **Crops**: development from warmth only (base `grow_tmin` 3 °C, full from `grow_topt` 13 °C — landraces suited to the climate; weeds hold it back a little; the winter crop near-dormant in winter); yield quality from soil water and weeds weighted over the growing days. The field planner expects each standing crop's own quality, else the village's recent harvests: a dry site clears more land (Trutok 13 → 20 fields in its first year).
4. **Fishing** (`fisher` role and `sk_fish` skill; a fisher for every boat in pick_role): a coastal village's boats belong to fisher households (a prime-age adult of each takes up the nets at founding). A household fishes in its own boat for what it needs: fresh fish for the pot (fresh fish at home lowers the wish), smoked fish laid in for the winter (late summer and autumn, `smoke_days` 8), its pantry or the village's stores running low. The catch grows with skill (0.55 + 0.6 x skill). Fresh fish is a house stock of its own, cooked first, spoiling 30% a day; the surplus is smoked at the hearth (3 FU an hour, wood 0.08 log a FU; smoked fish spoils 0.4% a day). Boats wear afloat (0.012 an hour) and rot a little ashore; owners mend them at the dock below 55% (a log from the woodstore, 2 hours); below 20% nobody rows out; a household with a fisher and no sound boat lays a new one on the stocks by the dock (5 logs, 30 work-hours; a building site like a house, launched as a new boat).
Numbers (seed 22, 54 days): fish 370-600 FU per coastal village (25-35% of the harvest); 165-257 FU smoked, 46-103 FU of fresh fish spoiled; boats mended 6-13 times; Tekheim built a third boat for a new fisher household; food conserved exactly every day; southern harvests 800-1,700 FU in their first year.
Not done: two-person crews, fish markets/sharing between households, squalls at sea, drying racks.

## D-101 · 2026-10-02 · R4: an economy of worth, news and gifts; the balanced pot (session 13)
Context: D-097/D-098's trade rested on invented wants (a village "short of fish" under 2 FU a person; wool wanted under 0.8 of the textile want): every food is the same FU to a stomach, so fish-for-grain had no real basis, and no village was ever short of wool because every village keeps sheep and grows flax (that is why wool never traded: no village wanted it).
Decision:
1. **The balanced pot** (`act.lua` cook): the perishables (meat, milk, fresh fish) first, then the keeping relish (smoked fish, cheese) up to the cook's share (`diet_p` 0.35), bread for the rest; each house's diet is its running relish share (`b.diet`). Bread with no relish (under `diet_lo` 0.12) or relish with no bread (over `diet_hi` 0.8) for weeks weakens people (health held to `diet_weak` 0.55) and adds `diet_hz` to the daily risk of death; they remember it. Households fetch bread when the house's bread runs low even with fish and meat in it (they had lived on fish and let the granary sit). Smoked fish fetched from the granary stays fish at home.
2. **Worth** (`sim/agents/econ.lua`): a good is worth `base x clamp(1.6 - have/want, 0.15, 1.6)` FU of grain a unit. Food and logs are the village's (granary, woodstore and larders together: grain against the sowing target of D-100; smoked fish and cheese as one relish against `fish_want` 3 FU a person; logs against the woodstore's want); wool and cloth a household's own (its whole pipeline, raw wool and flax to cloth, against `textile_hh_want` 1.5 GE a member). What is beyond `spare_k` 1.2 x the want can go.
3. **News, not omniscience**: a village knows another only by what its people saw there or heard from visitors (`s.know[o]`: what the neediest there would give, what those with plenty would take, how much is to spare, and when), learnt on every arrival both ways and at the founding; it fades toward a neutral guess (a middling price, half a load to spare) over `know_fade` 48 days, so old news sends a trader out to look and a wasted trip teaches.
4. **The exchange** (`econ.best_trade` each morning; `econ.deals` on arrival): for each good X a household (or the village) has to spare and each reachable partner, the good H to bring back is the one it values most against what the partner (by its news) would let go for: ratio = (worth of X there x worth of H here) / (worth of X here x worth of H there); the price is the geometric middle (k = sqrt(aX bX / aH sH) of H a unit of X: both gain); the trip goes when the gain (x (1 + trust/2)) beats its days (`trade_hour` 0.06 FU an hour of each person). On arrival the trader works out the deals from what is there now: the village's stores for pooled goods (paid from the granary, the woodstore, or the larder holding the most relish), the neediest households for wool and cloth (paying in their own goods or their share of the stores), each taking as much as is still worth the price to them; nothing wanted means the goods walk home ("Nobody at X wanted our Y at a price worth it now"). Each deal is a step at the buyer's door (`WORK.deal`); payment kept elsewhere is loaded there (`PICK.owed`); the trader's goods ride in a pack (`p.pack`, drawn as a sack), counted in the home village's food ledger.
5. **Relations**: `s.rel[o]` (trust, deals, gifts, lost); each deal kept adds `trust_deal` both ways; trust weighs the choice of partner. Households remember their partners (`hh.partners`).
6. **Sharing in the village**: a household with more than `share_keep` x its want of relish, wool or cloth carries some to one with under `share_short` of its own (kin first, those who gave to it before): a gift both remember (`hh.got`, `hh.gave`), bonding the people who met.
7. **Production answers worth**: fishing's eagerness scales with what more fish is worth (the village's own want, or what a neighbour would give less the carrying: `s.fish_worth`, set each settlement hour).
Fixes found by following traders: a daytime "exhausted" sleep lasted until the next morning (23 hours of fasting after a voyage: now `nap_h` 4); nobody goes to bed starving with a meal at home; a trip's end was skipped when the last drop had empty hands (the trader re-planned the trip forever and starved); a trip that cannot end at home is given up (`replans`); voyages and minding a boat tire like travel, not like work.

Numbers (shipped world, two game years): 31 exchanges, all walked or sailed: Moomoo and Upper Linamist trading fish, grain, logs and cloth both ways as their stocks swing; Hottoodingle Hop (no dock) wool and cloth for Zurug's fish and cheese by road; 12-63 gifts a village; diets 0.37-0.48 relish; no deaths from hunger.
