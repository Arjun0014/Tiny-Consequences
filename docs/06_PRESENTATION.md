# 06 — Presentation: look, sound, writing

The game is rated on Presentation separately. The planet is the interface, so the look *is* the UX.

---

## 1. Visual direction

**Cute toy planet in a bright sky.** Think tabletop diorama: soft low-poly shapes, saturated but gentle colours, chunky props slightly too big for the planet, a glowing atmosphere, puffy clouds. Tiny Skies is the mood reference (bright cyan sky, yellow glow near the horizon, dense little trees). Never copy its code or assets.

Contrast is the joke: it looks adorable while terrible things happen.

Biome colours blend smoothly across cell borders; add slight per-vertex noise so terrain isn't flat colour.

### Planet rendering targets

- **Terrain**: smooth-shaded icosphere (render level 5, ~10k vertices), elevation displaced and slightly exaggerated (mountains read clearly, coasts step down). Vertex colours from the sim, updated incrementally: only vertices of cells marked dirty (display-relevant change) are rewritten, at most ~4 times a second.
- **Ocean**: separate sphere at sea-level radius (it grows/shrinks with `sea_level`). Shader: depth tint (baked seabed depth per vertex), gentle vertex waves, fresnel highlight, coastal foam band, oil tint and sea ice from per-vertex values updated like the terrain.
- **Atmosphere**: slightly larger back-face sphere with a fresnel glow (cyan → white rim).
- **Sky**: full-screen gradient behind everything, with a few slow drifting background cloud streaks.
- **Lighting**: toon Lambert with 3 soft bands + rim light. The sun follows the camera at a fixed offset, so the visible side is always lit (legibility over realism).
- **Clouds**: a pool of 64–128 puffy cloud props placed where `moisture` is high, drifting with the wind, interpolated between sim updates. Rain = short streak quads under raining clouds; snow = white particles.

### Props (low-poly, generated by script)

Generate models procedurally (a small Node script writing glTF) so the pipeline is autonomous and consistent. All props share **one material** and **one small palette texture** (UVs point into palette swatches) so Defold can batch them. Use vertex colours only if you've verified Defold's glTF importer keeps them.

Needed: pine tree, round tree, palm tree, bush, grass tuft, rock, crop rows patch, hut, house, big house, ruin, fishing boat, tanker, deer, wolf, fish, person (body + head; job tints), volcano cone with crater, cloud puff, meteor rock.

Scale (planet radius R = 10): trees 0.12–0.2, houses ~0.12, tanker ~0.35, deer ~0.06, people ~0.04. People and animals only show when zoomed in enough to read them (distance LOD); trees and houses show at mid zoom; at full-planet zoom, terrain colour does the work.

Props sit on the surface: position = surface point, up = surface normal, random yaw. No floating, no z-fighting.

### Effects (VFX)

Lightning (bolt flash + sparks), fire (flame particles + smoke; glowing terrain vertices on burning cells), rain streaks, snow, drought shimmer, oil bloom (dark sheen spreading on the ocean), blight (crops turn purple-brown), meteor (streak, flash, shockwave ring, dust cloud), eruption (lava glow, ash plume), quake (short local camera shake + dust), tsunami (a ring wave that sweeps across ocean cells in the event's arrival order), new island (rises with steam), births (tiny heart pop), deaths (tiny ghost rising and fading), migration (a line of little people walking), village founded (houses pop in with bounce), village abandoned (houses crumble to ruins).

"Reduce motion" setting disables camera shake and flashing.

### Emotes over villages

Small icons projected from world to screen (GUI layer): content 😊, worried 😟, hungry 🍞, grieving 🕯, celebrating 🎉. At most ~30 on screen, fade by distance.

## 2. UI style

Cream rounded panels with soft shadows, chunky rounded display font, big icons, short text. Everything clickable has a hover state. Fonts (open licence, from github.com/google/fonts): **Fredoka** for titles/numbers, **Nunito** for text. Include their OFL licence files in `assets/fonts/`.

The chain popup is the most important UI element: large, clear icons, one line per link, year on the left, gold for player links, teal for nature.

## 3. Audio

- **SFX**: generate procedurally (a Node script synthesising WAVs, jsfxr-style) for: UI click, power cast (one per power), lightning, fire crackle loop, rain loop, splash, meteor impact, rumble (quake/eruption), wave, birth pop, death "poof", village founded chime, chain popup sting, goal complete fanfare.
- **Music**: one calm ambient loop. If the human drops a track into `assets_inbox/` (e.g. from the Ovani free sampler that jam participants receive), move it to `assets/audio/music/`, credit it in `assets/CREDITS.md`, and use it; otherwise ship with ambience only (wind, sea) and a music-off state. Never download music from unknown sources.
- Mix: music quiet under SFX; SFX volume scales down at high speeds (don't machine-gun sounds at ⏩).

## 4. Writing: voice and tone

**Dark comedy, cute delivery.** Villagers are small, earnest, slightly doomed and full of opinions. They know about "the Sky" (the player) and mostly misunderstand it. The chronicle is a tabloid.

Rules:
- Lines ≤ 90 characters. Punchy.
- No real people, brands, places or politics. The Tanker Company is fictional.
- No slurs, no sexual content, no jokes about suicide or self-harm, nothing mocking disability, ethnicity or religion. Death is treated absurdly and gently, never gorily.
- Children's lines are odd and sweet, never grim.
- The climate/butterfly message comes through irony and consequences, never lectures.

### Dialogue data format (`content/dialogue.lua`)

```lua
{ id = "fisher_oil_memory", weight = 5,
  when = { job = "fisher", memory = "oil_beached" },
  text = "Ever since the black tide in Year {memory.year}, the fish taste like coins." },
```

Condition keys: `job`, `age_band` (child/adult/elder), `trait`, `hunger` (">60"), `health`, `mood`, `season`, `biome`, `village_state` (thriving/struggling/famine/new), `memory` (event kind), `power_nearby` (a power used within 3 cells in the last year), `weather` (rain/drought/snow/heat), `partner` (has/lost), `children`, `era`, `global` (hot/cold).
Slots: `{name}`, `{partner}`, `{village}`, `{region}`, `{year}`, `{memory.year}`, `{memory.region}`, `{species}`, `{god}`.
Selection: filter by conditions → prefer more specific matches × weight → avoid the person's last 3 lines.

Targets: ≥ 300 lines in Chapter 2 across ≥ 40 condition groups; at least a third reference a memory or the Sky. Generated with AI assistance is fine; note it in the credits.

## 5. Naming

- Each continent gets a phoneme set (a "culture"): villager and village names are generated from it, so places feel coherent.
- Regions: adjective + noun ("Sapphire Sea", "Ashen Wood", "Grumbling Peaks", "Whistling Plains").
- Volcanoes: affectionate ("Old Grumpus", "Mount Hiccup").
- Tankers: "Good Intentions", "Probably Fine", "Minor Setback", "Oops Again".
- God names (title screen, editable): adjective + noun ("Humble Thunder", "Sleepy Comet", "Petty Monsoon").
- Keep all name lists in `content/names.lua`.

## 6. Art self-review checklist (use on every screenshot pass)

1. Full-planet view: continents, oceans, ice caps, deserts, forests, farmland distinguishable at a glance.
2. Cute: soft colours, rounded props, no harsh pure black except oil.
3. Scale: villages read as clusters at mid zoom; people readable at close zoom.
4. Props on the surface, aligned to the normal; no floating, no z-fighting, no clipping into terrain.
5. Lit side faces the camera; rim glow visible; nothing muddy or dark.
6. Clouds never hide more than ~30% of the visible planet.
7. UI legible at 1280×720 and 1920×1080, no overlap, text large enough.
8. Chain popup readable at a glance.
9. Year 0 vs Year 50 screenshots: obvious visible differences.
10. Performance overlay within budget (05_ARCHITECTURE.md §9).
