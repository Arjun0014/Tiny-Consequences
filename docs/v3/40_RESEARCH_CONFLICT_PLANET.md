## A. Small-scale conflict and cooperation

### A1. Why people take things
- **Need and drought.** Among the Turkana most raiders name hunger and drought as their first
  motive; livestock violence is higher in dry months, drier years and months drier than expected.
  Pastoralists raid "to restock a herd depleted or wiped out by disease or drought" ("We cannot die
  of hunger here when other men are herding livestock across the escarpment!"). Raids also
  redistribute after losses (McCabe; Gray et al. 2003, *Current Anthropology* 44:
  https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086/377669; Ndikumana/Schilling,
  "Raiding pastoral livelihoods", https://www.researchgate.net/publication/257883594; McCabe,
  "Livestock raiding among the Turkana: redistribution, predation and the links to famine",
  https://www.researchgate.net/publication/229463441).
- **Wealth and marriage.** Among the Pokot the motives are wealth and bridewealth (same sources);
  among the Nyangatom bridewealth is 30-60 cattle and raiders gain cattle and standing, though
  captured cattle are not quickly turned into a marriage (Glowacki & Wrangham 2013,
  https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12110-013-9178-8; 2015 PNAS,
  https://www.pnas.org/doi/full/10.1073/pnas.1412287112).
- **Medieval villages.** Hanawalt's ~20,000 English cases 1300-1348: theft rose with grain prices;
  much of it small food stolen from neighbours (bread from ovens, garden vegetables), often by
  women; burglaries peaked in winter when goods were stored in houses and barns; homicides peaked
  at planting and harvest, when people competed for the same work and land
  (https://www.hup.harvard.edu/catalog.php?isbn=9780674175808;
  https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/03044181.2023.2250952).
- **Implication**: taking is a choice weighed from *need* (a household's food against its mouths),
  *the gain* (what the thing is worth to that household now), *the chance of being seen and what
  follows*, and *restraint* (who they are taking from). No "criminal" type; scarcity raises it, ties
  lower it, a known herd nearby makes it possible.

### A2. Why mobile wealth makes people quick to answer harm
- Herding wealth walks away overnight; with no third party to appeal to, herders keep a reputation
  for swift retaliation as deterrence (Nisbett & Cohen's culture of honour; global test: Cao, Enke et
  al., "Herding, warfare and a culture of honor", NBER w29250,
  https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w29250/w29250.pdf; "Farmers, herders and fishers:
  the ecology of revenge", https://www.researchgate.net/publication/254215531).
- **Implication**: what is stolen matters (beasts more than grain in a store); a household that lost
  beasts guards them and answers; a household that answered is less often robbed (others *know*
  it answers: reputation is knowledge of deeds, carried as news).

### A3. Who joins, and why they hold together
- Raiding parties form along **social network** ties (friends and age-mates), not at random
  (Glowacki, Isakov, Wrangham, Mcdermott, Fowler & Christakis 2016, PNAS 113,
  https://www.pnas.org/doi/full/10.1073/pnas.1610961113).
- Large Turkana raids hold together through punishment of cowards and deserters by the community
  (Mathew & Boyd 2011 PNAS, http://www.sarahmathew.net/uploads/5/1/2/3/51236989/2011_mathewboyd_pnas.pdf;
  2014 EHB "The cost of cowardice"). Most small raids are stealth raids, not battles.
- **Implication**: a party is people who already like each other (`rel_v` affinity), kin and
  age-mates; each decides for their own reasons; nobody is assigned. Flight is common; death is rare
  and enormous for a small group.

### A4. Revenge, the feud and its end
- **Collective liability** ("social substitutability", Kelly 2000, *Warless Societies and the Origin
  of War*, https://www.researchgate.net/publication/37703900): once any member of a group answers for
  the others, retaliation can fall on anyone of the offender's group - the step from punishment to
  feud to war. Roscoe 2023 revisits it (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12110-023-09465-z).
- **The feud has paths** (Boehm 1984, *Blood Revenge*, Montenegro,
  https://archive.org/details/bloodrevengeanth00unse): each blow is a decision that can escalate or
  end it; elders press for a truce where the two sides live close and need each other; feuds were
  not fought inside a clan.
- **Peace in the feud** (Gluckman 1955, *Past and Present* 8): cross-cutting ties - marriages
  between the groups, kin in both, shared residence and dependence - divide loyalties and push for
  settlement (https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/9781503627116-039/html).
- **Compensation**: blood money/wergild exists in almost all feuding societies; paying it admits
  fault and buys peace; in Iceland it became routine and carried little stigma; payment in livestock
  (a cow ~ 2.5 ounce-units) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weregild; Byock, "Feuding in Viking-age
  Iceland's great village", https://viking.ucla.edu/publications/articles/feuding_viking_age_iceland_byock_vengeance.pdf).
- Revenge raids follow recent losses of people (Pokot/Tepeth after Turkana raids; Schilling et al.).
- **Implication**: a wrong is remembered as a specific deed (who, what, when, how known); it is
  *settled* by return or payment, *answered* by a blow in kind, *outweighed* by ties (kin, marriage,
  trade, help received) or *forgotten* slowly. Each blow is a fresh decision from present state; the
  same primitives give one forgiven theft or a long feud.

### A5. Defence and aggregation
- Warfare drives settlement **aggregation** and defensive works (palisaded hilltop Iroquoian
  villages; walled Puebloan sites; buffer zones between enemies) (LeBlanc; Allen & Arkush;
  https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1016063710831;
  https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10816-025-09760-2).
- **Implication**: watching beasts, gathering them in, carrying arms, building a wall and moving to
  a bigger place are each household or village choices weighed against labour, timber and the
  losses remembered - not a counter.

### A6. Social storage and hospitality (project)
- Exchange and obligations turn a neighbour's surplus into insurance (Halstead & O'Shea 1989); a
  refused request is itself remembered (v2 D-057/D-066 used refused help as grievance).
- Hospitality: guests fed at the host's store already exist (D-097, D-144); sanctuary (a guest is not
  harmed) follows from the same deeds: harming a guest is a deed known to everyone present.

## B. Living planet and emergent response

### B1. How people respond to sudden danger
- **Protective Action Decision Model** (Lindell & Perry 2012,
  https://www.researchgate.net/publication/51234490): environmental cues (sights, sounds, smells) and
  social cues (what others do) start the process; exposure -> attention -> interpretation; then threat
  perception, which protective actions are possible and work, and what others expect; behaviour is
  information search, protective action or emotional coping, shaped by facilitators and impediments.
- **Social attachment, not panic** (Mawson 2005, *Psychiatry* 68,
  https://www.csap.cam.ac.uk/media/uploads/files/1/mawson-2005-emotional-attachments.pdf): the usual
  response to danger is to seek familiar people and places - flight *toward* attachment figures;
  separation from them is a greater stressor than the danger; their presence calms. Mass panic is
  rare (Quarantelli; Drury).
- **The first rescuers are neighbours**: 60-100% of trapped earthquake victims were dug out by
  relatives, neighbours and villagers (Italy 1980: 90%; Armenia 1988: 95%); ~94% of those rescued
  alive were reached within 24 h, mostly with hands and simple tools
  (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7745699/).
- **Implication**: a person who perceives danger first goes to their dependants and kin (a parent to
  the children, a child home or to a parent), then away from the danger; the able and bold may face
  it (drive off, fight a fire, dig someone out); help comes from whoever is near and close. No
  "evacuation order" and no panic state.

### B2. Lightning
- 93% of lightning deaths are rural; farming is the main activity at the time of death, then being
  in a dwelling; most in open fields during rain; historically ~3 deaths per million a year (1800s)
  when work was manual and outdoors (Holle,
  https://www.vaisala.com/sites/default/files/documents/Annual_rates_of_lightning_fatalities_by_country.pdf;
  "Lightning-caused deaths and injuries related to agriculture",
  https://www.vaisala.com/sites/default/files/documents/Ron%20Holle.%20Lightning-caused%20Deaths%20and%20Injuries.pdf).
- **Implication**: lightning strikes where storms are, prefers tall and exposed things (trees, a
  person in a field), harms what is there; whether fire follows depends on what it hit and how dry it
  is. Exposure is the work schedule, not a dice roll on a village.

### B3. Fire and animals
- Ungulates and predators flee fires and travel far; they return when the burn greens up; forage
  peaks 1-15 years after; the weeks after a fire (no food, no cover, predators hunting the exposed)
  kill more animals than the flames (Ganz et al. 2022, *J. Anim. Ecol.*,
  https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1365-2656.13810;
  https://www.swfireconsortium.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/260313_Wildlife_fire_synthesis.pdf).
- **Implication**: animals need only "danger seen -> away" and "food here?" - range shift, predators
  following prey and the green-up follow from the existing herd/pack ecology (fauna.lua already
  avoids fire cells and kills in them).

### B4. Cold, snow and the dzud
- A **dzud** is a summer drought (thin pasture and hay) followed by a hard winter (deep snow, ice
  crust, cold); mortality is starvation (forage under snow/ice), freezing and weakness; drought +
  dzud kills more than either (https://www.researchgate.net/publication/280102584;
  https://www.undrr.org/understanding-disaster-risk/terminology/hips/mh0503).
- Deer winter survival tracks snow depth more than temperature (movement cost, food under snow);
  deer leave for winter ranges as snow deepens (80% gone by ~10 cm in one study; elk move downhill at
  ~60 cm) (https://doi.org/10.1071/wr25008; https://cdnsciencepub.com/doi/abs/10.1139/z95-127).
- **Implication**: snow depth on a map (from the planet's snow cover) is one physical field read by
  walking speed, forage reach, hay demand and herd movement; a dry summer's thin hay and a deep
  winter combine through the existing hay and culling logic - nothing dzud-specific.

### B5. Volcanoes
- **Laki 1783-84**: fluorine in ash poisoned grass; ~60% of Iceland's grazing livestock died
  (fluorosis); ~22% of the people died of famine and disease in the "Haze Famine"; the sulphur haze
  crossed the hemisphere and the following winter was among the harshest in centuries
  (https://www.encyclopedie-environnement.org/en/society/laki-fissure-eruption-1783-1784-2/;
  disaster aid: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0377027319305220).
- **Tambora 1815**: ~60 Tg SO2 -> stratospheric sulphate -> 1816 "year without a summer": crop
  failures, grain prices several-fold, emigration (Kandlbauer et al. 2013,
  https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/2013JD019767).
- **Implication**: an eruption is three physical things - local destruction (lava/heat/fire near
  the vent), ash on the ground downwind (grass poisoned or buried, roofs loaded, fertility later),
  aerosol in the sky (global cooling: the planet already carries `aerosol` into every cell's
  temperature, which v3 crops and pasture already read).

### B6. Earthquakes
- Building collapse caused ~75% of earthquake deaths in the last century; adobe and rubble masonry
  are the most vulnerable, timber frames among the best (http://doi.org/10.3390/buildings11050200;
  https://www.humanitarianlibrary.org/sites/default/files/2014/02/WCEE_NotesOnTheSeismicAdequacyOfVernacularBuildings.pdf).
- **Implication**: shaking at a place (from a static plate boundary's stress release) damages each
  building by its construction and condition; people are hurt by what falls on them (inside, or by
  walls); rescue by neighbours (B1). Steep tiles can slide and close ways.

### B7. Drought, prices and entitlement
- Sen (1981): pastoralists' food entitlement fell 84-92% in the 1970s Ethiopian drought as animals
  died and their price fell while grain rose; distress sales let the richer buy cheap
  (https://openknowledge.fao.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/b78a169e-40dc-46e7-b536-80a151772833/content/x5439e02.htm).
- **Implication**: v3 already values goods by each household's want (econ.lua); drought lowers
  pasture and harvests; households kill or sell beasts; the terms of trade should move by themselves.

### B8. Ocean temperature, rain and fish
- ENSO: weakened trades move warm water east; teleconnections double tropical drought extent in a
  strong El Niño; upwelling stops, plankton crash, fish die or move (anchoveta collapses 1972 with
  overfishing) (https://www.nature.com/articles/s43017-025-00742-2;
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_Peruvian_anchoveta_crisis).
- **Implication**: the planet already has SST-dependent evaporation (moisture.lua), an evaporation
  wobble (multi-year wet/dry spells), plankton by SST and upwelling, fish by plankton (ocean.lua), and
  v3 fishers catch from `F.fish` - an SST anomaly already reaches fishers' catches through fields.

## C. What the research says to build (summary)
1. **Specific deeds, known by specific people** (A4, A2, B1): harm and help as records with actors,
   victims, what, when and how each person knows - grievance, gratitude, reputation and blame are
   read from them.
2. **One danger model** (B1-B6): a danger is a physical thing at a place with a reach; it is noticed
   by sight and sound according to distance and state (asleep, indoors, night); noticing interrupts.
3. **Protective choice with attachment** (B1): dependants first, then away/shelter; the bold and
   able may confront, fight fire, rescue; help comes from the near and close.
4. **Injury** (A3, B2, B6): small, reusable: hurt slows, stops work, needs care, heals or kills.
5. **Things that break** (B5, B6, A5): buildings, bridges, boats, stores have condition and contents
   a hazard physically reduces; repair and rebuilding are ordinary work.
6. **Need, gain, risk, restraint** (A1-A3): taking is weighed by a household's need, the thing's
   worth to it, the chance of being seen, ties to the owners, and the person's boldness.
7. **Ties that pull both ways** (A4, A6): kin, marriage, trade, debts and help received restrain
   harm and press for settlement; their absence frees it.
8. **Planet fields read locally** (B4-B8): snow depth, heat/cold, dryness, ash, water - read by
   walking, forage, crops and fire; most already reach v3 through temperature and soil water.
