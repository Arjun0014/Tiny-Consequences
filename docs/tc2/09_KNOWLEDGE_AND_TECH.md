# TC2-09 — Knowledge and technology

Principle: technology is knowledge carried by real people (later also by institutions and records),
applied with real materials, tools and skill. There is no tech tree to unlock; there are techniques that
exist in heads, spread by contact and die with their last practitioners. An "Iron Age" is what historians
call a time when iron tools are in common use.

## 1. Techniques (data)
`Technique { id, name, domain (food, material, building, craft, medicine, navigation, war, social),
prerequisites: techniques known, materials available, tools; difficulty (practice hours to competence);
complexity (how many skilled models a learner needs: Henrich); affordances (actions, recipes, building
parts, boat kinds it enables); decay (forgetting rate without practice) }`.
Examples of a chain: fire -> charcoal; clay + fire -> pottery -> kiln; ore known (seen at an outcrop) +
charcoal + furnace (clay, bellows) -> smelting -> forging -> iron tools; timber framing -> stone footings ->
masonry -> towers.

## 2. Carriers and proficiency
Each person holds techniques with proficiency 0..1 (side table). Practice raises it; disuse decays it.
Some knowledge is **of places** (where the ore is, the ford, the good fishing ground) and is likewise carried.

## 3. Transmission
- Teaching during co-activity: parents to children, masters to apprentices (an apprenticeship is a
  relationship and a personal history event), work partners; learning rate by teacher proficiency,
  learner aptitude and time together; complex techniques need several proficient models nearby.
- Observation: watching a skilled person work (lower rate); traded objects carry hints (a bronze knife
  reveals that bronze exists, not how to make it).
- Movement: marriage, migration, captives and travellers carry their knowledge to new places.

## 4. Discovery (the adjacent possible)
When a person repeatedly performs activities that bring the ingredients of an undiscovered technique
together (materials, tools, situations, related techniques), a small keyed chance per practice-hour,
scaled by curiosity and skill, yields the technique (Arthur & Polak: invention as combination).
Discovery is a historical event caused by the activities and conditions that made it possible.

## 5. Loss
When the last proficient holders in a connected population die or forget, the technique is lost there
(a historical event). Small isolated groups lose complex techniques first (Henrich 2004).

## 6. Phase
F4. F0 has no techniques beyond implicit farming and woodcutting.
